\section{Foundation choice}\label{sec:foundation}

As mentioned, this document is going to contain two parts, where \zcref{prt:theory} works \emph{externally} from the semantics perspective and proves the validity of the quasi-coherence principle in a variety of topoi, and~\zcref{prt:appl} works \emph{internally} in various classifying topoi where we show the quasi-coherence principle for different theories have different applications in branches of synthetic mathematics.

However, in terms of the mathematical development within the two parts, especially the choice of the back ground logical system, they can be put on an equal footing, by working within a constructive meta-theory. More concretely, we work uniformly in the Bishop-style constructive mathematics, that can be interpreted in the internal language of \emph{any} topos. 

In addition to the usual constructive internal language of topoi, where every quantifier is assumed to be bounded by some small set, we also allow \emph{unbounded quantifiers}. Semantically, this is supported by the \emph{stack semantics} developed by~\citet{shulman2010stack}, which is an extension of the usual Kripke-Joyal semantics that can additionally supports the interpretation of unbounded quantifiers over all sets internally in a topos. Effectively, this means we have a category $\Set$ of (small) sets, and we can make sense of statements of the form: ``For any set $X\in\Set$, \ldots'' In~\zcref{prt:appl} and also in~\zcref{sec:internalduality} where we mainly work internally in a topos $\mc E$ different than the base topos $\Set$, we will also use the explicitly different notation $\mb S$ to refer to the category of sets internally in $\mc E$.

One reason we need a small dose of unbounded quantification in our development is to formulate constructively e.g.\ the notion of \emph{internal projectivity} in a topos. We thus also take the opportunity to mention that, throughout this document we will assume that \emph{countable choice} holds, i.e.\ the natural numbers object $\N$ is projective in the base category $\Set$ of sets. In many stream of constructive mathematics this is indeed considered as a valid principle, though its validity is also often debated in the literature; we refer the interested reader to the chapter by~\citet{richman_countable}. 

In $\Set$ there is also a universe of \emph{propositions}, which we denote as $\pp$. We will identify a proposition with its \emph{extension}, viz.\ as a subset of the terminal set $\mb 1$. We do assume $\pp$ is \emph{impredicative}, in the sense that quantifying over all sets still produces a proposition in the universe $\pp$. Internally in an arbitrary topos, this form of impredicativity is indeed supported by the stack semantics. Similar to the case of $\Set$, when we work within the internal language of a topos, we will often write the universe of propositions as $\Omega$, which is the standard notation for the subobject classifier in a topos.
